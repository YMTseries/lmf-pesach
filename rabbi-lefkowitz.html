Rabbi Simcha Lefkowitz

Founder of the Leon Mayer Fund. Rabbi Emeritus of Congregation Anshei Chesed. Fifty years of building things that outlast him.

More than thirty years ago, Rabbi Simcha Lefkowitz and Shlomo Mayer started a fund in memory of Shlomo's father, Leon. The idea was simple and it has never changed: when a family in the community hits a wall, someone should be able to help them quietly, before anyone else finds out there was a wall.

Rabbi Lefkowitz has administered that fund ever since. He has never taken a salary for it. For the last twenty-five years he has done the work from a wheelchair.

The Fund has no offices and no payroll — which is another way of saying that the overhead you don't pay is the time he donates.

The Leon Mayer Fund is not the only thing he built. In 1984 he co-founded Labor & Industry For Education, Inc. (LIFE), a full-service not-for-profit social services agency running programs across New York City and Nassau County; he became its Executive Director in 2004. From 1981 to 2004 he served as Associate Dean and Chief Administrative Officer of Yeshivas Toras Chaim, where enrollment grew past 500 students and more than doubled during his tenure. There he oversaw high school regents certification and fundraising, and supervised construction of a $3 million early childhood center and a $4 million high school building.

Before that, from 1974 to 1981, he was Associate Dean of the Los Angeles Kollel. He received semicha from Bais Medrash Gevoah of Lakewood in 1972, completing a minor in Educational Pedagogy alongside his studies.

He is a departmental chaplain for the Nassau County Police Department, and Rabbi Emeritus of Congregation Anshei Chesed in Hewlett — where he still gives his Gemara shiur.
